## 2. THE FOUR FINDINGS THAT ACTUALLY MATTER

### Finding 1 — The anti-viral product is the one we can build on

> Comfrt Dreamer Blanket: $676K product GMV in one week. **Its single best video made $1,115 —
> 0.16% of total product GMV.** It runs on 2,751 creators producing a high volume of
> modest-performing content. (z.media, week ending 4 Oct 2026)

This is the whole thesis in one data point. You do not need a viral hit. You need a product with
**distributed creator volume**, and you manufacture that volume with AI. A brand that can generate
500 competent product videos a week is playing a game Amazon sellers structurally cannot enter.

### Finding 2 — Creator count is a vanity metric, and it fails publicly

> Mellow Sleep MarshMellow Comforter: **9,400 creators — the most on the chart — sold 1,979
> units. That is $37 per creator.** (z.media)

Versus KakaPeople: **119 creators, $2,597 per creator.** Versus Comfrt: $246/creator.

**The number to optimise is revenue-per-creator, not creator count.** Any content engine must be
measured that way or it will generate a thousand videos and sell nothing.

### Finding 3 — Generic hardware is a price race, and the price is the race

Dr.Flash sells a handheld garment steamer at **$29.70** and moves 44,800 units a month off it.
There is no spec sheet that justifies $89. **An own-brand website cannot win a commodity
comparison** — it can only win by being a different offer.

> This is the single most important line in this document. If product #1 is a generic appliance
> sold on its spec sheet, this business does not exist. It must be sold as a system.

### Finding 4 — Validate three ways, and enter late

> Triangulate Amazon Movers & Shakers, TikTok hashtag growth, and Google Trends. All three should
> show upward momentum before committing capital. Catching a trend at **week 4–8 of its lifecycle
> (not day 1)** is the safest launch window. Start with **200–500 units** to test velocity, then
> reorder aggressively. (Ainfluencer, Jul 2026)

Corollary from the data: half of the September top 10 were **selling slower at month-end than
their 30-day average**. Every brand on the October 4 chart fell week-on-week. Trends decay. The
inventory bet is small and the re-order decision is weekly.

## 3. THE DECISION

**Product #1 — a handheld garment steamer, sold as a system, not a spec.**

Chosen because:

- **Demand is proven at depth, not as a spike.** 44,800 units in 30 days off one product, 37,989
  reviews, 4.6★. That is a multi-year asset, not a trend.
- **The content format is native to the product.** Wrinkle-removal is a visible before/after
  transformation — the same format the data credits for teeth whitening ("one of TikTok's most
  consistently high-engagement content types"), cleaning ("satisfying cleaning content"), and
  cooking ("visible transformation in seconds").
- **It is genuinely sourceable.** Live Alibaba/made-in-china quotes, below.
- **Low regulatory risk.** It is an appliance, not a cosmetic or a supplement. No FDA, no ingest
  claims, no health claims. This matters enormously — 52% of the proven TikTok GMV is beauty and
  supplements, and we are deliberately walking away from that money because the compliance and
  claim-substantiation burden would kill a brand we cannot afford to defend.

Assumes the $89 price point holds, which is the thing that must be proved (§4).

commission, and a creator commission — inside $20, on a price we do not control.**

That comparison is the entire reason to own the endpoint. It is also why §4 is not optional.

## 4. WHAT WILL KILL IT — stated before we spend money

1. **Commodity creep.** If we ship a white-box clone and list it as a "2000W garment steamer
   with 300ml tank," we are competing with $29.70 sellers and we lose. **Mitigation: the product
   is sold as a bundle + service + brand, never as a spec.** Non-negotiable.
2. **Returns.** Steamers leak, scale, and fail. Expect 8–12% return rate, not 3%. The margin
   survives it; a thin-margin version does not.
3. **Certification.** Requires cUL/ETL (Canada) and relevant US marks. Non-negotiable, budgeted.
4. **Trend decay.** Half the top 10 decelerated into month-end. This is a 90-day window, not a
   business.
5. **The content engine is the product.** If we can only make 10 good videos a month, this is an
   Amazon listing with extra steps. Everything else is secondary.

## 5. THE SCALE PLAN — one product, then the config

The answer to "one product or as many as we can squeeze" is **neither, yet: one product, built as
a template, with the second product costing config rather than code.**

| Stage | What exists | Cost to add product #2 |
|---|---|---|
| #1 (now) | The template: owned endpoint, funnel, capture, content engine, fulfilment path | — |
| #2–#10 | Same code, new product config | ~1 day each, no new pages |
| #11–#100 | Same, self-serve onboarding | a form, not a build |

Each product gets its **own endpoint, own funnel, own content, own margin profile** — which is
the actual moat. Amazon's weakness is that its 100k SKUs share one listing template and one
customer relationship. Ours is the inverse: infinite endpoints, one per product, each owned.

**Queue for #2–#10, chosen from the same dataset on the same criteria (generic, sourceable,
visual-transformation content, no regulatory risk):** cordless rechargeable heat brush ($74.90
AOV, "more revenue than three $15 products combined" — Kalodata), cordless rechargeable vacuum
(Trucozie, growth doubled), standing oscillating fan (steady, non-spiky), mini carpet cleaner.
Beauty and supplements are **excluded on regulatory grounds** despite being 52% of the GMV.
