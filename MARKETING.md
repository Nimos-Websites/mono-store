## 1. POSITIONING

**For** people who have already bought a $29.70 steamer and found out what it was ·
**a** garment steamer sold as a system — unit, case, protocol, three-year warranty ·
**that** you own instead of having been sold to.

| | Category leader | MONO |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $29.70 | $89 |
| What it is | Appliance | Appliance + case + protocol + warranty |
| Reviews | 37,989 at 4.6 | None. It is new. |

**The one-line differentiator:**

> The appliance is deliberately almost identical to the $29.70 one. That is the point — we are
> not pretending the hardware is special. We are selling what happens after you unbox it.

## 2. THE ICP — one person

**The burnt-out steamer owner.** 28–48, has already bought one, it dripped, scaled, or stopped
heating in eight months. They are not looking for a gadget. They are looking for the thing that
does not make them buy it again. **This is our entire market and they are reachable in one
sentence: "Which one didn't break."**

**Explicitly not the ICP:** first-time buyers shopping on price, anyone wanting a garment presser,
anyone outside North America/UK/EU (certification cost kills those markets at this margin).

## 4. THE FUNNEL — four states

```
SEE ───────────► CONSIDER ───────────► BUY ───────────► OWN
TikTok /        /steamer: render,     $89, no          3-year warranty,
search          full spec, protocol   account needed   re-order case parts
```

**One conversion goal per page.** `/` and `/how` and `/catalogue` point at `/steamer`. `/steamer`
is the only page with a form. Two competing CTAs is how a one-product store converts on neither.

**No discount mechanic. Ever.** The first hundred customers set the reference price permanently.
A discount on a $89 appliance bought by people who already own a broken one reads as panic.

## 5. CHANNELS — ranked

**Tier 1 — where this exact person already is.**

| Channel | Why it fits | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Search: "why does my garment steamer leak" / "garment steamer stopped working"** | High intent, low competition, and the query IS our product | The single best channel. A page that answers the failure mode sells the replacement. |
| **Review-recovery content** | The category leader's 4.1–4.6 ratings are the opening | Every video is "here's what the cheap one does at month eight, here's ours." Not slander — mechanism. |
| **Warranty/retailer forums, Reddit** | Where owners actually complain | Answer with mechanism, not with a link. Link only after you've answered. |
| **Search: "handheld steamer for [travel/dense fabric/linen]"** | Bottom-funnel, cheap | Long-tail content pages. One per use case. |

**Tier 2 — volume content, automated.**

TikTok Shop / Reels / Shorts, per PRODUCT-DECISION.md: wrinkle removal is a visible
transformation, and the evidence says the winner is **volume, not a hero video** — the category
leader's single best video earned 0.16% of its revenue. **Target metric is revenue-per-creator,
not creator count.** Mellow Sleep had 9,400 creators and $37 each. Do not repeat that.

**Tier 3 — do not.**

Amazon (we would be the 37,989th review and price-compressed), influencer unboxing (product is
small and cheap, the format needs a big reveal), paid social prospecting (no visual hook at $89
that beats a $29.70 listing), Facebook groups spam.

## 6. THE 90-DAY PLAN

### Days 1–30 — build the proof
Site finished and audited. **No selling.** The failure mode for a one-product brand is launching
before the page can carry the argument. Exit criteria: every number traces to source; the
disclosure page is airtight; the render is labelled a render.

### Days 31–60 — own the failure content
20 pieces of failure-mode content (leaks, scale, dead element, cord fraying — the four real
failure modes). Plus the long-tail search pages. **250 units ordered. Nothing more.**
Exit criteria: ≥15 pieces live, ≥40 organic sessions/day, first 10 units sold.

### Days 61–90 — open, and re-order on evidence
Open the buy box publicly. Publish the return rate as it comes in, in public, whatever it is.
Exit criteria: 250 units sold or a clear reason they didn't move.

**Kill criteria, decided now.** If day 90 shows <60 units sold at a ≥8% return rate, the product
is wrong, not the marketing. Do not add a second SKU to fix a failing first one. Fix the offer or
kill it and go to `/catalogue` queue #2.

**Scale criterion.** If we sell 250 units at a ≤8% return rate and ≥50% gross margin, order 1,000
and start the content engine in earnest. Below that, do not scale.

## 7. METRICS — six numbers

| Metric | Day 90 target | Why it's the one |
|---|---|---|
| Units sold | ≥60 of 250 | Demand exists |
| Gross margin | ≥50% | It survived COGS, freight, payment, 3PL |
| Return rate | ≤8% | The real killer for steam appliances |
| Revenue per content piece | > $15 | The volume thesis, measured honestly |
| Organic sessions/day | ≥40 by day 90 | The owned channel is working |
| Customer-list signups | ≥150 | The asset the marketplace can't take |

Explicitly not goals: impressions, follower count, add-to-carts, revenue-per-creator vanity
(measured as a diagnostic, not chased).

---

## 8. WHAT WOULD KILL THIS

1. **Commodity creep.** Listing an identical appliance at $89 and hoping. The whole plan is
   contingent on never appearing next to a $29.70 listing with the same spec.
2. **Returns over 12%.** Steamers leak and scale. At 12%+ with our margin the unit economics
   invert and no amount of content saves it.
3. **Buying inventory off a spike.** Half the category's top sellers decelerated into month-end.
   250 units, weekly re-order decision, no exceptions.
4. **Certification skipped to save time.** cUL/ETL is not optional in Canada. Shipping
   uncertified is a customs seizure, not a fine.
5. **Adding product #2 before product #1 works.** The whole thesis is that one product done
   properly is the template. Adding a second failing product tells you nothing.

## 9. THE HONEST PART

MONO is a concept brand. There is no workshop, no supplier relationship, no 250 units and no
customer. Every market figure on the site is a third-party platform estimate retrieved
8 October 2026 and every sourcing figure is a live supplier quote from the same date. Both shift.

What this document is actually for is the part that survives: **the diagnosis of why generic
hardware can't be sold on a spec sheet**, the decision to enter the trend at week 4–8 rather than
day 1, the choice of a product whose content format is a visible transformation, the explicit
return-rate budgeting, and the revenue-per-creator metric that tells you whether an AI content
engine is real or just expensive.

Hand MONO a signed supplier quote and the only things that change are the render and the copy
that describes a company which does not yet exist.
